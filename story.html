OUR STORY

So.

I have this interest in books, right, I like to read 'em. I also like designing stuff, heck it's what I went to college for, and I like all the different aspects of book design taken on their own, and especially when brought together. I like researching books and authors. I was a bookdealer for ten years, and during that time was able to handle and sell some incredible books.

Also, I like to find obscure books. It's just in my nature, I guess; maybe my millenial hipster inclinations. That many years of looking through people's collections of books, or their parent's collections of books, you come across all kinds. Of books and people.

A bunch of those obscure books I find are obscure because they are long out of print.

Sometimes they're also not very good, or just super duper dated. But there are a lot of books in the world that get overlooked and sometimes they just need a push. A reissue. Someone enthusiastic to promote 'em.

So that's where I come in.

Years ago I attended The Denver Publishing Institute. It was incredible for a number of reasons. I was able to meet Joyce Meskis, long-time owner of Denver's legendary Tattered Cover (I wonder what she would have thought about it being bought up by Barnes & Noble, and thus no longer being indie). I was also able to meet Gary Groth, legendary publisher behind Fantagraphics. What else even happened during that course. Right: a lot of information about how the publishing industry works from a variety of speakers/working professionals, how diverse the opportunities can be, how to get started in it, ...the whole bit.

I never did seriously pursue that career. One reason is because I wasn't interested in being an editor, which is what 95% of the people taking the course were working towards, and I really wasn't comfortable with the idea of trying to make it in New York City on entry level salary. Everybody said, if you want to make a lot of money, don't work in publishing. You don't have to work in NYC, but it sure helps if you want to work for the big publishers. These days thankfully there are remote jobs available, but still, they don't pay great.

It took me a punishingly long time before I realized, wait: I can publish on my own and have full control of what I publish. I can publish public domain works so I don't have to deal with authors. Authors are, taken as a whole, great people, but let's keep it simple here. There are resources for everything I need, most of them for minimal or zero cost.

Thus, Curiouser House Publishing was born.

The OUR is a bit royal, as really it's just me doing everything. Hi. I'm Patrick Reinhart. The layout, cover design, interior design, typography, research, sourcing photos and incidental materials, publishing through KDP and IngramSpark, and setting up this website. It's why it takes a while to produce new books. That and the full-time job working nights.
